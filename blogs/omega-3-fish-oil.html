Omega-3 Fish Oil: The Essential Nutrient for Heart, Brain & Joint Health

Updated July 2026 7 min read Aiva Wellness Editorial Team
Fact-checked & sourced from peer-reviewed research

The body cannot make omega-3 fatty acids on its own — they have to come from food or a supplement. Among the different types, EPA and DHA from fish oil have the deepest body of research behind them, touching everything from heart rhythm to brain function to joint comfort.

What Exactly Is Omega-3 Fish Oil?

Omega-3s are a family of polyunsaturated fats that the human body needs but cannot produce on its own. Fish oil is one of the richest natural sources of two of the most studied omega-3s: EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid) and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid). A third type, ALA (alpha-linolenic acid), comes from plant sources like flaxseed and walnuts, but the body converts only a small fraction of ALA into EPA and DHA — which is why fish-derived omega-3s are considered the more direct source.

Fatty fish such as salmon, mackerel, sardines, and trout naturally contain EPA and DHA, along with shellfish like mussels and oysters. Fish oil supplements concentrate these fatty acids into a convenient daily capsule, which is useful for anyone who doesn't regularly eat fish two or more times a week.

How Omega-3 Supports Heart, Brain & Joints

Omega-3 fatty acids are structurally built into cell membranes throughout the body, which is part of why their influence spans so many systems at once — most notably the heart, the brain, and the joints.

  • Heart health — EPA and DHA are well known for supporting healthy triglyceride levels, a key factor in cardiovascular wellness, and are recommended by health bodies such as the American Heart Association as part of a heart-conscious lifestyle.
  • Brain health — DHA is a major structural component of brain tissue and plays an important role in brain development and maintenance across the lifespan, which is why it's linked to mental clarity and cognitive wellbeing.
  • Joint comfort — omega-3s help the body produce specialised compounds involved in regulating the body's natural inflammatory response, which is one reason they're often discussed alongside joint comfort and mobility.
  • Naturally derived — sourced from fish, with a long history of dietary use in populations known for strong cardiovascular health.

"You can't out-supplement a poor diet, but omega-3s are one of the few nutrients where the science consistently points the same direction — heart, brain, and joints all benefit from getting enough."

What the Research Shows

Omega-3 fish oil is one of the most researched supplements in the world, though — as with most nutrition science — results vary depending on the population studied and the dose used. Here's a balanced look at where the evidence currently stands:

250-500 mg
General Health Dose
Commonly cited daily intake of combined EPA and DHA for general wellness in healthy adults.
3 g/day
Blood Pressure Research
A 2022 analysis suggested around 3 grams of combined EPA and DHA per day — roughly what's found in 4-5 oz of salmon — may help support already-normal blood pressure.
Mixed
Large Cardiovascular Trials
Some major trials found omega-3 supplements reduced cardiovascular events in at-risk groups, while others found little difference versus placebo — researchers note formulation and dose likely matter.

In short: the most consistent evidence supports omega-3's role in maintaining healthy triglyceride levels, while findings on broader cardiovascular event prevention in generally healthy people are more mixed. Eating fatty fish regularly remains one of the most reliably supported dietary patterns for cardiovascular wellness.

How Much Omega-3 You Need

There's no single official RDA for EPA and DHA, but several expert bodies offer useful reference points. For general health, a combined 250-500 mg of EPA and DHA per day is commonly cited as sufficient for most healthy adults. People focused on specific goals, such as supporting already-elevated triglycerides, may see research using higher amounts — generally under a doctor's guidance.

Since a typical fish oil softgel provides around 300 mg of combined EPA and DHA, checking the label for the actual EPA/DHA content — not just the total fish oil weight — is the most useful way to compare products and doses.

This article is for general educational purposes and is not medical advice. Fish oil can have a mild blood-thinning effect at higher doses and may interact with blood-thinning medications. Please consult your doctor before starting any new supplement, particularly if you are pregnant, breastfeeding, on medication, or managing an existing health condition. Always follow the dosage on the product label.

Choosing a Quality Omega-3 Supplement

Not all fish oil capsules are created equal. When choosing a supplement, look for:

  • Clear EPA and DHA content — so you know exactly how much of the active fatty acids you're getting per serving, not just the total fish oil weight.
  • Purity and freshness testing — for heavy metals, oxidation, and rancidity, since fish oil can degrade over time.
  • Third-party tested manufacturing — for purity, safety, and consistency batch after batch.
  • Sustainable sourcing — transparent brands disclose where their fish oil is sourced and how it's processed.

The Takeaway

Omega-3 fish oil remains one of the most consistently studied nutrients for heart, brain, and joint health. While it isn't a substitute for a balanced diet, it's a well-supported way to help close the gap for anyone who doesn't regularly eat fatty fish — and a conversation worth having with your doctor if cardiovascular or cognitive wellness is on your radar.
